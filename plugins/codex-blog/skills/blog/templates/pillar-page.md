# Template: Pillar Page

## Output contract

- `image_mode=deferred` is the default. Do not generate, request, plan, or insert images or visual instructions unless the user explicitly enables images.
- Every square-bracket drafting token below is an instruction or variable, never literal article text. Replace it with final copy/data or delete it; real Markdown links and citations are unaffected. Never emit image, visual, internal-link, TODO, or other drafting markers. With images deferred, omit visual instructions completely.
- Add an internal link only when a verified destination URL is supplied. If a relevant destination is unknown, omit the link from the article and record the opportunity in the delivery report.
- When images are explicitly enabled, replace optional visual guidance with real accessible assets; never publish the guidance itself.

**Description:** Comprehensive topic authority guide that serves as the central hub for a topic cluster. Pillar pages provide broad, thorough coverage of a subject and link out to supporting articles that dive deeper into sub-topics.

**Target Word Count:** 3,000-4,000 words

---

## When to Use This Template

- **Content Goal:** Establish topical authority and create an SEO hub that ranks for high-volume head terms
- **Search Intent:** Informational (users want a complete understanding of a topic)
- **Funnel Stage:** Top-of-funnel awareness and mid-funnel education
- **Use When:**
  - Targeting a broad, competitive keyword with high search volume
  - You have (or plan to create) 5+ supporting articles on sub-topics
  - The topic is complex enough to warrant 3,000+ words of coverage
  - You want to build a content cluster architecture around this topic

## Section-by-Section Structure

---

### Title

**Format:** "The Complete Guide to [Topic] in [Year]"

**Guidelines:**
- Include primary keyword naturally
- The year signals freshness and improves CTR
- Keep under 60 characters for full SERP display

```
# The Complete Guide to [Topic] in [Year]
```

Optional image guidance, only when images are explicitly enabled: Hero image that visually represents the topic. Use a custom graphic or high-quality photo - not a generic stock image. Alt text should include the primary keyword.

---

### TL;DR Box (concise; length follows the material)

[ANSWER-FIRST] Provide a concise summary that directly answers the searcher's core question. A reader should be able to stop here and walk away informed. This box improves dwell time by immediately delivering value and earns featured snippet potential.

```
> **TL;DR:** [One to two sentences defining the topic and its importance.] [One sentence on the key takeaway or most surprising finding.] [One sentence on what action to take.] [STAT: key statistic that grounds the summary]
```

---

### Table of Contents

Auto-generated from H2 headings. Use anchor links for jump navigation. This improves UX on long-form content and can generate sitelinks in SERPs.


### Introduction (200-300 words)

[ANSWER-FIRST] Open with a clear, direct statement about what [Topic] is and why it matters right now. Do not bury the lead with backstory or fluff.

**Structure:**
1. **Opening hook** (1-2 sentences): Lead with a compelling statistic or trend that creates urgency
   - [STAT: current year statistic showing the scale or growth of this topic]
2. **Topic overview** (2-3 sentences): Define the topic clearly for someone encountering it for the first time
3. **Relevance statement** (1-2 sentences): Why this matters right now - what has changed recently?
4. **What the reader will learn** (2-3 sentences): Preview the guide's coverage - set expectations so the reader commits to reading
5. **Credibility indicator** (1 sentence): Why should the reader trust this guide? (experience, data sources, methodology)

[INFO-GAIN: personal experience] Include direct experience only when the author
supplies methodology, evidence, and results. Otherwise state the research basis
and source set without first-person framing.

Internal-link opportunity, only when a verified destination URL is supplied: If you have a related "Why [Topic] Matters" article, link it from the relevance statement.

### What Is [Topic]? (200-300 words)

[ANSWER-FIRST] Start with a one-sentence definition that could serve as a featured snippet. Keep it jargon-free for accessibility.

**Structure:**
1. **Clear definition** (1-2 sentences): Simple, authoritative definition of the topic
2. **Context and scope** (2-3 sentences): Where this fits in the broader landscape - what category does it belong to?
3. **Key components** (3-5 bullet points): Break the topic into its essential parts or characteristics
4. **Common misconceptions** (1-2 sentences): Address what this topic is NOT to prevent confusion
5. **Brief history or evolution** (2-3 sentences): How the topic has developed to its current state

Optional visual guidance, only when images are explicitly enabled (chart-type): Include a diagram or infographic showing the key components and how they relate to each other. Suggested: labeled diagram, flowchart, or concept map.

[STAT: definition-supporting data point] Include at least one data point that contextualizes the topic's scope or importance.

Internal-link opportunity, only when a verified destination URL is supplied: Link key terms to their own dedicated articles if they exist in the cluster.

### Why [Topic] Matters (200-300 words)

[ANSWER-FIRST] Lead with the single most compelling reason this topic deserves attention. Quantify the impact wherever possible.

**Structure:**
1. **Primary impact statement** (1-2 sentences): The most important reason backed by data
   - [STAT: impact or ROI statistic]
2. **Industry trends** (2-3 sentences): Market data showing growth, adoption, or shifting priorities
   - [STAT: trend or growth statistic]
3. **Consequences of ignoring** (2-3 sentences): What happens if you do not engage with this topic?
4. **Opportunity framing** (2-3 sentences): What is possible if you get this right?
5. **Who this affects** (1-2 sentences): Specific roles, industries, or use cases

Optional visual guidance, only when images are explicitly enabled (chart-type): Bar chart or line graph showing growth trend, adoption curve, or impact data over time.

[INFO-GAIN: unique analysis] Include a data point or trend observation that is not commonly cited - something from your own research or analysis.

Internal-link opportunity, only when a verified destination URL is supplied: Link to case studies or data-driven articles that expand on the impact claims.

### Core Sections (6-8 H2s, 300-400 words each)

Each core section covers a major sub-topic within the pillar. These sections should be comprehensive enough to stand alone but concise enough that a dedicated supporting article can go deeper.

**For each core section, use this structure:**

```
## [Sub-topic as H2 - written as a clear, keyword-rich heading]
```

[ANSWER-FIRST] Open with a 1-2 sentence answer or definition of this sub-topic before elaborating.

**Structure per section:**
1. **Direct answer or definition** (1-2 sentences): What is this sub-topic and why does it matter within the broader topic?
2. **Key principles or steps** (150-200 words): The essential knowledge, broken into:
   - Numbered steps (for processes)
   - Bullet points (for principles or characteristics)
   - Sub-headings H3 (for complex sub-topics)
3. **Example or evidence** (50-100 words): A specific, concrete example that illustrates the concept
   - [INFO-GAIN: real-world example] Use a first-hand example, case study, or original observation when possible
4. **Common mistakes or pitfalls** (50-75 words): What do people get wrong about this sub-topic?
5. **Transition to deeper content** (1-2 sentences): Bridge to the next section or to a supporting article

[STAT when useful: include a verified sub-topic-specific data point only when
it materially improves the section.]

Optional image guidance, only when images are explicitly enabled: Where applicable, include screenshots, process diagrams, or example visuals that support the explanation.

Internal-link opportunity, only when a verified destination URL is supplied: Link to the dedicated supporting article for this sub-topic. Use contextual anchor text (not "click here" or "read more").

**Repeat this structure for each of the 6-8 core sections.**

### Advanced: [Expert-Level Sub-topic] (200-300 words)

[ANSWER-FIRST] Signal immediately that this section is for experienced practitioners, then deliver an advanced insight they will not find in beginner guides.

**Structure:**
1. **Audience gate** (1 sentence): "If you're already [doing X], here's how to [achieve Y]."
2. **Advanced insight** (100-150 words): A technique, framework, or perspective that goes beyond standard advice
3. **Evidence or application** (50-75 words): Where this has been applied successfully
4. **Caution or prerequisite** (25-50 words): What you need to have in place before attempting this

[INFO-GAIN: expert knowledge] Differentiate with supported original material
when it is genuinely available, such as documented direct experience,
proprietary data with methodology, or attributable expert interviews. A
research-only article may instead contribute distinctive synthesis, comparison,
or application without implying first-hand experience.

Internal-link opportunity, only when a verified destination URL is supplied: Link to advanced-level supporting articles or case studies.

[INFO-GAIN: personal experience] Note personal tool use only when the author

Internal-link opportunity, only when a verified destination URL is supplied: Link to any dedicated tool reviews or comparison articles in the cluster.
